SPARSE REWIRING · DOWN_PROJ INTERPRETATION · 26 NOV 2026

What do the surviving neurons write?

Because sparse L0 rewiring froze every MLP neuron’s output column (down_proj), the 18 surviving neurons across Rank 0 and Rank 1 could only steer Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct along pre-existing output directions. We decode all 18 frozen vectors using Logit Lens and three Jacobian-lens variants (J-Lens, J++, R-Lens) to see what concepts and tokens they control.

What these results mean in plain English

In short, decoding the 18 frozen down_proj vectors strongly backs up your Section 7 hypothesis — that the 90%+ validation CE recovery alongside the accuracy drop (80.5% → 67%) is driven primarily by style & length compression rather than new math learning:

  • The 5 shared neurons (L11–L19) are formatting & stopping switches: L16 #5569 sits directly between markdown/colon structure (:**, :\n\n, [*) on +v and <|endoftext|> (stopping, −7.8σ) on −v; L17 #7054 controls LaTeX/heading delimiters (##\n\n, \[, :\n\n); L19 #5606 separates underscore/markup formatting (+2.9σ to +8.2σ on every delimiter) from plain English prose & contractions ('m, 'd, 'll).
  • Both arms independently grabbed digit/number knobs right before the output (L27): Rank 0 picked L27 #7926 (digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 9 on +v) + L27 #4914 (spelled-out English numbers twenty..ninety on −v); Rank 1 picked L27 #6214 (all 10 ASCII digits 0–9 cleanly on −v) + L26 #8751 (Left, Done).
  • Functional overlap exceeds 5/18: Even where the two arms picked different neuron indices, they picked cross-layer substitutes — most clearly Rank 0’s L12 #6760 ↔ Rank 1’s L6 #5466 (R-Lens cos = +0.66, both controlling paragraph breaks vs. single-newline endings).
  • Why CE dropped 90%+ while accuracy fell (80.5% → 67%): Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct naturally writes long markdown/LaTeX explanations (~220 tokens), whereas GSM8K gold answers are terse (~130 tokens). Flipping roughly a dozen pre-existing formatting, stopping, and digit neurons is enough to match gold’s short style (huge CE win) while cutting reasoning short (accuracy drop).

Five Big Takeaways (including Geometry & Cross-Lens Checks)

  1. Strong confirmation of the Section 7 hypothesis (style/formatting + conciseness > math): Almost the entire shared core (L11 #8235, L16 #5569, L17 #7054, L19 #5606) controls markdown/LaTeX delimiters, EOS (<|endoftext|>), newlines, and prose/contractions, while both arms independently discovered L27 digit/number neurons (L27 #7926, L27 #4914, L27 #6214) and L26 #8751 (Left/Done).
  2. Genuine cross-layer substitutes between the two arms: The single strongest pair across all 18 neurons is Rank 0’s L12 #6760 ↔ Rank 1’s L6 #5466 (raw +0.40, J-Lens +0.67, R-Lens +0.66, J++ +0.45). Excluding the 5 shared neurons completely, the 7 Rank-0-only and 6 Rank-1-only subspaces still share 2 strong common directions in R-Lens space (top principal-angle cosines 0.78 and 0.58, vs. only 0.40 and 0.18 in raw Logit Lens). Conversely, L27 pairs have low full-vocab cosine (−0.24 to +0.14) even though L27 #7926 and L27 #6214 both hit digits 0–9 in their top-10 — because 10 digits are only 0.006% of the 152k vocab, showing why both token tables and heatmaps are needed.
  3. Shared L17 pair (#6230 vs. #7054) is completely orthogonal (raw +0.009, R-Lens +0.042): Neither duplicates nor push-pull opposites — #7054 writes markdown/LaTeX delimiters (##\n\n, \[, :\n\n) while #6230 writes assertion/inequality tokens (≠, ≥, 确实, (True). Keeping both under Rank 1’s shared input read vector a suggests both are triggered by the same GSM8K context while steering orthogonal output axes.
  4. No high-norm / massive-activation bias — in fact the opposite: All four L27 survivors (1.3%–10.5% norm %ile), plus shared L11 #8235 (1.5%) and L19 #5606 (1.2%), sit in the bottom 1–10% of their layer’s down_proj norm distribution (medians: 18.9% Rank-0-only, 8.6% Rank-1-only).
  5. Cross-lens behavior (R-Lens was the cleanest overall): Logit Lens degrades heavily at L2–L6 (cos(LL, R) = 0.13–0.28) before converging by L26–L27 (0.99–1.00). Early-layer win: on L3 #574, both J++ and R-Lens agree at cos = 0.97 and cleanly surface math/teacher/solutions (数学, Maths, 老师, rozwiązania), whereas plain J-Lens and Logit Lens showed noise. Two J++ caveats to watch: a sign flip on L18 #4890 (cos(J++, R) = −0.32 while LL/J/R agree at 0.77–0.94) and heavy attenuation on L26 #8751 (gain = 0.26 while LL/J/R agree at 0.99).

1 · Setup & Method

In the production sparse-rewiring run, only the input read rows (gate_proj, up_proj) of each MLP neuron were allowed to change; the write matrix down_proj remained frozen at its base Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct weights. Whenever surviving neuron j in layer L changes its activation by Δa_j(x), it injects a scalar multiple of the fixed vector v_j = W_down^(L)[:, j] ∈ ℝ^1536 into the post-block residual stream.

v_j = W_down^(L)[:, j] # frozen output column in ℝ^1536
u_j = J_lens^(L) · v_j # linear transport L → L27 (identity for Logit Lens & L27)
u_readout = (u_j / RMS(u_j)) ⊙ γ_final # unit-RMS scale + final RMSNorm gain
logits_j = W_U · u_readout − mean(W_U · u_readout) # project to 151,936 vocab tokens, mean-center
z_j(w) = logits_j(w) / std(logits_j) # standardized vocab z-score (+v promoted, −v suppressed)

Logit Lens

No transport (J = I). Direct projection through final RMSNorm gain & W_U. Exact at L27, noisy in early layers.

J-Lens (jlens)

Corpus-averaged input–output Jacobian E[∂h_27 / ∂h_L] over Wikitext. Transports intermediate vectors to final-layer coordinates.

J++ Lens (jpp)

Jacobian-filtered variant (Ayonrinde & Lindsey, 2026) designed to remove early-layer noise and improve workspace coherence.

R-Lens (rlens)

LRP-modified backward pass (Blank, Bhatia & Nanda, 2026). Cleanest early-layer fidelity and highest cross-depth consistency here.

Single-token columns have zero aggregation; multi-variant columns (e.g., digits 0–9 or ["####", " ####"]) average the exact z-scores of their surface variants.

8 · Scope Caveats & Not Done Yet

While the 3σ–15σ alignment with formatting, newlines, LaTeX, EOS, and digits strongly supports the style-compression explanation for the 90%+ CE recovery and −14 pt accuracy drop, static down_proj decoding alone cannot prove “zero reasoning”: several neurons (L3 #574, L17 #6230, L4 #1398) point to domain/assertion cues, a single 1,536-dim vector can be polysemantic, and the rewired gate_proj/up_proj inputs still control when each output fires.

1. Activation shifts & active pole sign (Δa_j on val128, GPU / sl-0)

Measure mean(Δa_j), positive/negative fraction, and top firing contexts for both arms. Critical for neurons like L19 #5606, which carries both unwanted markdown (** +8.2σ, \[ +6.0σ) and wanted #### (+5.7σ) on +v—it may flip from negative during CoT to positive at answer termination.

2. Leave-one-out causal ablations (GPU / sl-0)

Turn off one survivor at a time and measure ΔVal-CE, Δlength, and Δaccuracy. Tests directly whether ablating 2–3 formatting/EOS switches (L16 #5569, L19 #5606, L12 #6760) recovers untuned accuracy (~80%).

3. In-distribution GSM8K finite-difference JVP (~1 min on A100)
