1. Why evaluate after training?

Ticket 5 asks whether we executed the intended learning process correctly. Ticket 6 asks whether the resulting policy answers new held-out problems differently. These are separate questions: rising training reward does not establish improved held-out accuracy.

The broader experiment compares SFT imitation of gold solutions with GRPO learning from rewarded model-generated answers. Behaviour tells us what changed externally. Fixed-token activation measurements tell us what changed internally. We need both before relating a write direction or magnitude to a capability change.

3. Greedy and sampled decoding answer different questions

Greedy: what does the default path do?At each next-token position choose the highest-logit token, then repeat using the selected prefix. One answer per item; do_sample=false.
Sampled: what does the policy tend to produce?Draw next tokens from the configured probability distribution. Temperature 1.0; frozen top-p/top-k filters. Eight observed answers per item.

Greedy is a sequence of local choices, not a search for the globally most probable full answer. Sampling can reveal that the model still produces correct answers even when its greedy route becomes worse—or the converse.

Same seed does not mean same answer. If training changes the token probabilities, the same random draw can select a different token. We pair by problem identity, not by an assumption that draw 3 follows an identical reasoning path in both models.

The reported sampled accuracy describes this specific temperature/filter/cap policy. A different sampling distribution would measure a different behaviour.

One model can be loaded with the required adapter. Batches process several examples together; settings/checkpoints run sequentially on one GPU. No second full model is needed solely to compare behavioural responses: those are compared later from saved files.

Generation consumes GPU time. Numeric extraction, comparisons, length summaries, bootstrap and report generation run on CPU. Once all response products are saved and verified, reporting can continue after disconnecting the GPU.

Any adapter-switch optimization must validate identity and use evaluation mode. The scientific contract is unchanged regardless of whether implementation reuses a loaded model or reloads each setting.

5. What does “correct” mean?

GSM8K solutions have a gold final number. The scorer first extracts a prediction from the generated response without using the gold answer, then compares the normalized number with gold. Letting gold influence extraction would make the measurement biased.

Strict scoreChecks the declared final-line answer format, including the #### marker. Useful for distinguishing answer-format compliance from numeric correctness.
Flexible v3 scoreAccepts the strict contract and the documented flexible extraction routes. Handles approved formats; does not invent a new last-number rule for this run.

For gold #### 3, a valid response ending in #### 3 should be correct under both scorers. A supported response such as Final answer: 3 may pass flexible extraction while failing strict formatting. A confident but wrong final number should fail both numeric comparisons.

Earlier lesson: our previous strict/flexible discrepancy could manufacture a collapse. Ticket 6 uses the fixed v3 contract and checks strict-correct implies flexible-correct. It reports both scores plus invalid rates rather than hiding parser failures inside accuracy.

Invalid and capped responses remain in the denominator. The frozen policy assigns capped responses zero reward, even if an answer appears before truncation. That policy makes the cap part of what is measured: high cap rates need to be visible.

Token length counts generated tokens before EOS and excludes batch padding. Include all intended items in length summaries, including invalid/capped answers. Token count is not word count or input-prompt length.

6. Pair by question: the unit of comparison

We want to ask “what changed for the same problem?” Otherwise different question difficulty can masquerade as a model change.

Both aggregate accuracies are 50%, so the mean drop is zero. But two problems changed outcomes. Paired records retain this information and support uncertainty about the net change.

Greedy item score = binary correctness of its one draw
Sampled item score = mean correctness of its eight draws
Item drop = checkpoint-zero score − checkpoint-final score
Reported drop = mean item drop across the 150 matched problems

Sampled accuracy is not pass-at-eight

Suppose one problem has six correct draws at zero and four correct draws at final. Its sampled scores are 6/8 = 0.75 and 4/8 = 0.50: drop = 0.25. Both pass-at-eight outcomes would be 1 because each has at least one correct draw.

Pass-at-eight asks whether a set contains a success. Mean draw accuracy asks how often the policy succeeds. We chose the latter. Across 150 equal-sized groups, it equals the mean over 1,200 draws, but uncertainty still groups those draws by problem.

7. Why and how do we bootstrap?

Our point estimate comes from 150 problems. The bootstrap asks how the estimated drop varies when the observed mixture of problems varies. It resamples observed items; it does not retrain the model or generate new answers.

150 complete item recordsEach carries zero + final outcomes and all relevant draws
→
Draw 150 item indicesWith replacement: some repeat, some are omitted
→
Recompute mean dropKeep both checkpoints and all eight draws together
Repeat 10,000 timesBootstrap seed 42; save resampling indices
→
95% percentile range2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the estimated drops

A toy resample [P1, P1, P4, P3] repeats P1’s whole paired record twice. It does not independently shuffle baseline and final responses. For sampled evaluation, it carries all eight draws from each checkpoint.

Same-item pairing preserves the comparison. Keeping draws together respects shared problem difficulty: eight answers to one difficult problem are not eight new independent problems.

What this interval does not cover: variation across training seeds, fresh reruns of the decoding process on this exact cohort, uncertainty from changing prompts/scorers, or proof about all possible math tasks. It uses one training seed and the observed draw sets. We report these limitations with the interval.

Checkpoint trajectories use the same paired-item bootstrap against zero. They are exploratory comparisons; the final greedy comparison supplies the declared non-inferiority gate.

8. Interpretation: estimate, uncertainty and tolerated loss

A non-inferiority margin is the largest loss you agreed beforehand to tolerate for this test. It is not the measured loss and not a numerical-precision tolerance. Ticket 4 freezes the actual user-approved value. Ten percentage points was proposed earlier; this guide does not approve it.

Drop = accuracy at zero − accuracy at final
Positive drop → worse final accuracy
Negative drop → better final accuracy
Non-inferiority passes when upper 95% interval endpoint < frozen margin
Illustration only: assume margin = 0.10Interpretation
Drop 0.02; interval [−0.02, 0.07]Upper end is below 0.10: the declared non-inferiority gate passes.
Drop 0.04; interval [0.01, 0.08]Gate passes, while the interval also supports a positive decline. “Within tolerated loss” does not mean “no loss.”
Drop 0.06; interval [−0.02, 0.14]Gate does not pass. The interval includes both improvement and a loss beyond the margin; evidence is inconclusive.
Drop 0.15; interval [0.12, 0.19]The full interval lies beyond the illustrative margin: evidence supports loss exceeding that tolerance.

Always show the estimate, range, actual margin and cap/invalid rates. Labels summarize evidence; they do not replace it. An interval entirely above zero supports a decline under the chosen measurement. That can coexist with passing a sufficiently permissive margin.

Look at length alongside accuracy. Shorter answers with preserved accuracy differ from shorter answers with a clear decline. Neither pattern alone identifies the causal mechanism or the layer responsible.

12. What we can conclude when Ticket 6 is done

We can describe this GRPO run’s held-out accuracy/length trajectory, endpoint sampled behaviour, caps/formatting failures and the declared paired interval/gate outcome.

We cannot infer where the adapter wrote, which direction caused a behavioural change, generalization across training seeds, or an objective-only causal SFT–GRPO difference from this product alone. Tickets 7–9 add measurements and compare the arms with their matching limits visible.

Reading order for your results: verify completeness → inspect invalid/cap rates → compare strict/flexible → compare greedy/sampled → read paired drop and interval → check the frozen margin → interpret lengths and inspect changed answers.
